# KEYS HAPPEN — Brand Marketing DNA
Version: 1.0 | Locked by founder: 2026-11-20 | Owner: MKT OS
Status: APPROVED CREATIVE DIRECTION; NOT PUBLICATION APPROVAL
Brand: Trusted Locksmith | Site: https://trustedlocksmithnearme.com
Canonical brand promise: **Keys happen. Have a plan. Pressure shouldn't.**

## Non-negotiable positioning
- Be a brand worth following **before** people need a locksmith.
- Build fond recognition and participation through everyday key mishaps. **Entertainment first; selling second.**
- Speak about preparedness, not fear, crime, break-ins or panic.
- Free household Keys Happen Plan is the primary useful CTA. It must be safe, without access secrets, login or coercive lead capture.
- Audience participation is central: ask viewers to tell true, funny stories **in comments**. Replies should acknowledge, riff gently, ask follow-up questions and never pressure them to buy.
- Encourage sharing with friends and household members without exposing addresses, key locations, entry codes, photos of identifiable keys or information that could compromise physical security.
- Proposed $30/year membership is **not open for sale**. It may be mentioned only as a future optional product, explicitly labeled proposed, and only when relevant; **never use a membership checkout CTA** while operational/legal gates remain closed.
- Do not promise dispatch, provider availability, membership eligibility, price protection or live service where not verified.
- B2C-led awareness / B2B-led initial monetization are distinct; do not confuse the two.
- Marketing does not change the stabilized website or merge/deploy code. Website Build Pro handles evidence-backed site interventions; QA/Release certifies deployment.
- Execution autonomy L2: draft, prepare and submit for approval; do not publish, spend or schedule absent authorization.

## Working editorial mix (HYPOTHESIS, not a measured optimization)
- 70% entertaining relatable stories and comedic sketches
- 25% helpful preparedness, free-plan education, safe reminders
- 5% subtle, accurate, **future-only** subscription context; no sales links until approved

## Growth loop
Relatable story -> comments/tags/shares -> discovers free Keys Happen Plan -> completes privately -> voluntarily shares safe invite/print copy -> new audience -> ongoing entertaining content.
Track distinct campaign IDs and site UTM tags when the landing destination is verified.
Primary metrics: qualified plan completions and voluntary plan shares; supporting: comment rate, meaningful comments, profile visits, landing visits and referral visits.
No vanity-reach-only success criterion.

## First experiment: KH-2026-001 — Everyone has a keys story
Decision: CREATE GROWTH TEST. Goal: learn which hook induces meaningful comments and completed free plans.
Creative variants are drafts; existing owned assets must be audited before new generation, and all imagery must be licensed/owned or AI-generated with disclosure where needed.
Deliver in vertical 9:16 for Reels/TikTok; safe-crop 4:5 still for feed. Warm cream, navy, lime palette. Human, candid, cinematic-light photography; realistic, not stock-slick; no fear, no distress. No visible addresses, lock mechanisms, detailed keys or security codes. Overlay brand-copy separately in Canva to keep typography consistent.

### A — The click
- Hook (on screen): **"The door clicks. Your keys are on the other side."**
- Visual image brief: Adult in everyday clothes outside a closed apartment door, empty pockets, split-second comic realization; casual daylight, warm navy/cream framing; no readable address or visible key; expression amused rather than distressed.
- Short video structure: 0–2s door closes (no instructional lock details); 2–5s pocket pat; 5–8s frozen reaction; 8–11s text "Keys happen"; 11–14s "What's your version? Tell us in the comments."
- Caption: "Everyone knows that sound. What happened the last time your keys had other plans? Tell us in the comments. #KeysHappen"
- Primary CTA: **Tell us your story in the comments**. Secondary profile/free-plan CTA only if link verified.
- Community follow-up: "The pocket pat is universal. What happened next?"

### B — The hiding place
- Hook: **"What is the weirdest place your keys have ever turned up?"**
- Visual image brief: Candid close-up of a set of generic house keys placed beside an everyday coffee cup on a kitchen counter, person laughing out of focus, playful composition, no key-identifying detail.
- Caption: "Fridge? Laundry basket? Somewhere that still makes no sense? We need the story. Funniest answers may inspire our next post—with permission. #KeysHappen"
- Primary CTA: **Comment the weirdest place**.
- Reply prompt: "Okay but HOW did they get there?" 
- Safety: Never ask people to reveal where they hide spare keys.

### C — The prepared friend
- Hook: **"Every friend group has one person with a plan. Is it you?"**
- Visual image brief: Two friends laughing in a bright apartment foyer, one shows a generic folded note (no legible personal details), atmosphere warm and humorous.
- Caption: "Tag the person who somehow always has a plan. Or the person who needs one. The free Keys Happen Plan is there when you're ready. #KeysHappen"
- Primary CTA: **Tag a friend**; secondary: make free plan, conditional on QA-verified link.
- Safety: No photo/visual of a real person's access information.

### D — The confession booth (comment-only low-cost test)
- Hook: **"Confession time: how many times have you gone back for your keys today?"**
- Visual: Navy typography on cream with one lime keyhole-style abstract accent. No photographic production required.
- Caption: "We won't judge. Our pockets are doing quality control. Tell us your number."
- Primary CTA: **Comment a number or story**.
- Hypothesis: text-only community post can outperform edited video on meaningful comments per 1,000 views.

### E — The plot twist (helpful bridge)
- Hook: **"Your future self called. It wants a key plan."**
- Visual: Unbranded paper checklist beside mug, keys and pen, clean morning light; no personal addresses or access locations.
- Caption: "You can't prevent every key mishap. You can make the next one less chaotic. There's a free plan for that—no signup, no access secrets. What's your one preparedness tip?"
- CTA: **Share a safe tip in comments**, secondary free plan after QA.
- Important: tips must not disclose entry bypass, spare-key hiding locations or security vulnerabilities.

## Test design and thresholds
- Before publishing: verify live plan URL, events, comment moderation and rights; classify account status; check channel suitability.
- Start with A/B/D rather than publishing all five at once. Hold creative format/channel/time comparable when feasible. Do not confuse a small test with causal proof.
- Hypothesis thresholds to review after >=100 qualified visits: >=20% free-plan completion rate among qualified visitors; >=10% voluntary share rate of completed plans. These are unvalidated working targets.
- Engagement: measure meaningful comments per 1,000 views and comment-to-visit rate; avoid invented industry benchmarks.
- SCALE / ITERATE / STOP / NEEDS_MORE_EVIDENCE / WEBSITE_INTERVENTION_REQUIRED.
- If the plan funnel underperforms despite qualified traffic, send an evidence-backed Website Build Pro request; do not alter the live site directly.
